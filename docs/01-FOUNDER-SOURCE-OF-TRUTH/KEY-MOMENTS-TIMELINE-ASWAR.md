## The Crucible Moments That Shaped a Founder

This timeline captures the pivotal experiences that transformed Aswar from a struggling student to a founder building world-class products from India.

---

## **1997-2006: The Foundation Years**

### 3rd Grade - 9th Grade: The Failing Student Who Won Hearts
**Years:** 1997-2005  
**What Happened:** Aswar failed subjects consistently—every exam, every year. But during those same years, he won the Moral Science Award annually (given to only one student per grade for character, discipline, and relationships).

**The Paradox:** Academic failure + Character excellence

**What It Taught Him:** Grades don't measure everything. How you treat people matters. Discipline and character create respect even when performance doesn't.

---

### 9th Grade: The Turning Point
**Year:** 2005  
**What Happened:** Aswar was made vice captain of his school team. The responsibility meant he couldn't afford to fail anymore—it would become news across the school. He put in the work and, for the first time in his academic life, passed all subjects.

**The Moment:** His class teacher made him stand in front of everyone and asked the class to congratulate him.

**That Night:** Aswar made a promise to himself: *"I cannot afford to fail anymore. I have to be genuine to my efforts."*

**What Changed:** He never failed another subject through graduation.

**What It Taught Him:** Hard work delivers results. Responsibility drives transformation. When you face the challenge instead of running from it, you can change everything.

---

### 10th Grade: The Head Boy Election
**Year:** 2006  
**What Happened:** In a school where only top academic rankers typically became Head Boy, Aswar was nominated as a candidate. He was shocked—he double-confirmed they really called his name. The students elected him Head Boy, beating all the top rankers.

**The Paradox:** The kid who used to fail subjects won the highest student leadership position.

**What It Taught Him:** Character and relationships matter more than credentials. People see potential even when grades don't reflect it. You can compete and win even when you're not the "obvious" choice.

---

## **2009-2013: The Professional Foundation**

### 2009: First Job, First Promotion
**Company:** Mastek (Mumbai)  
**Role:** Trainee Software Engineer → Software Engineer  
**What Happened:** Trained in Oracle Database and .NET, deployed to US insurance billing software. Promoted within the first year due to commitment and outcome-first approach.

**What It Taught Him:** Outcomes matter. Deliver results, and recognition follows.

---

### 2013: The Unintentional Career Pivot
**Decision Point:** Leave Mastek for Advisory Board Company  
**Why:** Mother diagnosed with brain tumor, needed to stay in Chennai. Mastek wanted him to return to Mumbai.

**The Choice:** Applied to multiple companies (Capgemini, CTS, HCL, Wipro, Polaris, Advisory Board). Selected by all. Chose Advisory Board because it was a **product company**, not a service company.

**What It Taught Him:** Sometimes life forces a decision, but you can still make it strategic. Product companies > service companies. That instinct was already there.

---

## **2013-2019: Falling in Love with Product Management**

### 2015-2016: The Moment He Fell in Love with Product Management
**Company:** Advisory Board Company / Optum  
**Role:** Senior Data Analyst / Senior Integration Analyst  
**The Problem:** Product Manager was stuck on provider directory cleanup for Crimson Medical Referrals (CMR). Outdated directory data led to failed patient referrals and revenue loss for practices. PM was trying to build cleanup functionality *inside* the CMR application.

**The Question Aswar Asked:** *"Why do you think provider clean activity should be within CMR application? Why can't it be outside as a separate operational task that feeds data to CMR?"*

**What He Built:** Separate backend application for provider directory cleanup, operationalized with external BPO vendor (MiraMed Ajuba). Customer-facing CMR stayed clean with updated data.

**The Outcome:** 
- Customers saw clean data, less administrative burden, could take action to reduce out-of-network referrals
- Aswar won **Delivery Excellence Award of the Year** at Advisory Board

**The Realization:** *"I can shape products. I can solve enterprise-scale problems. I can build things that create real impact."*

**What It Taught Him:** Product management is about asking the right questions, thinking outside the box, then executing inside reality. Impact matters more than output.

---

### 2016-2018: Building Teams, Shaping Products
**Role:** Senior Business Analyst  
**What Happened:** 
- Formed first-ever Business Analyst team in Chennai
- Grew team to 10 members (5 for new customer implementation, 5 for existing customer support)
- Played crucial role in product development alongside Product Manager
- Set up offshore operations team with MiraMed Ajuba

**What It Taught Him:** He could lead teams, build systems, and shape products at scale. Product management wasn't just a dream—it was his calling.

---

### 2018-2019: The Culture Shift
**What Happened:** Optum acquired Advisory Board. Culture changed from employee-first, innovation-friendly to corporate hierarchy. Advisory products shut down to promote Optum products. Learning stalled.

**The Decision:** *"The moment I realized I'm not learning anything new, I knew it was time to move."*

**The Criteria for Next Move:**
- Must be a healthcare company
- Must be a Product Manager or Product Analyst role
- Must help build products that solve real-world problems

**What It Taught Him:** Culture matters. Learning matters. When growth stops, it's time to move.

---

## **2019-2025: The athenahealth Transformation**

### 2019: The Crucible Moment (US Trip)
**Company:** athenahealth  
**Role:** Product Manager, Interface Product  
**What Happened:** Within 3 months, traveled to athena's US headquarters for knowledge transfer on DIRECT Messaging product. But the current PM had been laid off. No knowledge transfer. No team. No roadmap.

**The Environment:** Rooms filled with IIT/IIM graduates with far more experience. Aswar felt inferior.

**The Breaking Point:** Senior manager told him to "be assertive." That night, he couldn't sleep.

**The Memory:** He remembered 9th grade. He remembered the Head Boy election. He told himself:

*"What has brought me here will not take me forward. There is something else I need to start focusing on."*

**The Strategy:** Spent 2 months observing, learning, adjusting. Didn't react—prepared.

**What It Taught Him:** Face the situation, don't run from it. Past success doesn't guarantee future success. You have to earn it again, every time.

---

### 2019: The Breakthrough (Leadership Presentation)
**The Moment:** First leadership meeting—presenting product updates to athena's leadership team across all zones.

**The Performance:** Flawless presentation. Answered every tactical question.

**The Manager's Response:** "I never expected you to present this well. Job well done."

**That Night:** Aswar felt the same pride he felt when his 9th-grade teacher made the class congratulate him. Hard work delivers results.

**The Shift:** Stopped seeing people as "IIM guy" or "IIT guy." Started seeing them as equals. Even if they were superior, he could still question them and seek clarity.

**What It Taught Him:** Confidence is earned through preparation and performance. Remove the labels. Focus on the work.

---

### 2019-2021: The Rescue (DIRECT Messaging Transformation)
**The Starting Point:**
- DIRECT Messaging built in 2015 purely for compliance (MU2/MU3)
- 5% send success rate
- No funding since 2017
- No active development or team

**What Aswar Did:**
- Formed new scrum team in Chennai
- Talked directly to customers, support teams, operations
- Prepared 3-year product roadmap, presented to leadership (widely accepted)

**Year 1 Results:** Send success rate from 5% to 30%

**Promotion:** Senior Product Manager (2021)

**Final Results:**
- 80%+ send success rate
- 90%+ receive success rate
- Replaced every fax touchpoint in athenaOne with DIRECT
- Saved massive fax costs for athena and customers
- Represented athena in DirectTrust forums, participated in working groups (Directory, TIM+, Referral 360x)

**What It Taught Him:** He could rescue abandoned products. He could transform failure into success. He could compete at the highest level.

---

### 2020-2021: Building from Scratch (Electronic Case Reporting)
**The Context:** Post-COVID need for real-time notification to Health Information Exchanges (HIEs)

**What Aswar Built:** Electronic Case Reporting (eCR) custom application by inheriting ONC eCRNOW open source app

**The Scale:** Largest eCR implementation in US healthcare (athena's customer base connects to almost all state HIEs)

**The Timeline:** Completed development and production rollout in under a year

**What It Taught Him:** He could build products from scratch, not just rescue existing ones. He could operate at massive scale.

---

### 2022-2025: Solving the Biggest Problem (Referral 360x)
**The Research:** Deep dive with UX research to understand athena's referral problems:
- Fax-based referrals instead of electronic
- Split experience (part fax, part DIRECT)
- No referral tracker, leaving ambiguity in patient care

**The Pitch:** Presented research findings and proposed solution to leadership. Approved for immediate execution.

**The Challenge:** Needed 15 developers, only 3 available from internal transfer. Recruited 12 contractors with HR and Engineering Manager.

**The Timeline:** June 2025 - December 2025 (6 months)

**The Launch:** Pilot started showing good results in February 2026. Actively recruiting more pilot customers for athena-Epic referral exchange using Referral 360x standards.

**The Conference:** Presented mockups to 200+ participants at athena's Thrive conference. Standing-room-only session, one of the top presentations. First time presenting on stage at that scale.

**What It Taught Him:** He could identify the biggest problems, pitch solutions to leadership, scale teams quickly, and deliver complex products on aggressive timelines.

---

## **2023-2024: The Validation (Tamil Nadu Government Award)**

### The Competition
**Challenge:** Government of Tamil Nadu seeking designs for mobile outreach vehicle for differently-abled persons in remote locations

**Participants:** 54 designs submitted, including well-recognized big MNCs in the design field

**Aswar's Approach:**
- Visited differently-abled hospitals to understand ground-level challenges
- Interviewed several physiotherapists
- Visited automobile coach factories to understand existing limitations and challenges
- Designed based on real user needs, not assumptions

**The Result:** **First place.** Aswar's design won, beating all the big MNCs.

**The Differentiator:** Big MNCs submitted designs without ground work. Aswar did the validation work.

**The Impact:** The mobile outreach vehicle is now built and running in remote locations of Tamil Nadu, funded by the World Bank through the Tamil Nadu government.

**The Realization:** *"Why can't I change and bring value to Indian healthcare? And if my idea fits the global market, why can't I build for the world?"*

**What It Taught Him:** Ground work beats brand names. First-generation graduates can compete with and beat big MNCs. He could be an entrepreneur.

## **The Pattern Across All Moments**

Looking at the timeline, a clear pattern emerges:

1. **Face a difficult situation** (failing subjects, no KT at athena, competing with MNCs)
2. **Feel inadequate or inferior** (not the top student, surrounded by IIT/IIM graduates, just starting vs. established MNCs)
3. **Remember past victories** (9th grade, Head Boy, previous successes)
4. **Commit to learning and hard work** ("What brought me here won't take me forward")
5. **Do the ground work others skip** (study hard, observe and learn, visit hospitals and interview users)
6. **Deliver results that exceed expectations** (pass all subjects, rescue DIRECT, win government award)
7. **Prove the doubters wrong** (become Head Boy, earn respect at athena, beat big MNCs)
8. **Level up to the next challenge** (next grade, next role, next company, next mission)

**The Core Belief Running Through Everything:**

*"I may not have it today, but I can learn it. One step at a time, I'll get there."*
