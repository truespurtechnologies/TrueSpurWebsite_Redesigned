## The Origin: A First-Generation Graduate's Journey

Aswar graduated with a Bachelor of Computer Science Engineering from BSA Crescent Engineering College in 2009. But his journey to that graduation was anything but conventional.

From 3rd grade through 9th grade, Aswar failed subjects consistently. Every exam, every year. Yet during those same years, he won the Moral Science Award—given to only one student per grade for character, discipline, and how they treated others. Every single year.

**The Turning Point (9th Grade):**
When Aswar was made vice captain of his school team in 9th grade, something shifted. The responsibility meant he couldn't afford to fail anymore—it would become news across the entire school. He put in the work. For the first time in his academic life, he passed all subjects.

His class teacher made him stand in front of everyone and asked the class to congratulate him. That moment changed everything.

That night, Aswar made a promise to himself: "I cannot afford to fail anymore. I have to be genuine to my efforts."

He never failed another subject through graduation. And then something remarkable happened: his fellow students elected him **Head Boy**—beating all the top academic rankers. In a school where only toppers typically won that position, the students chose character over grades.

## The Professional Foundation: Learning Healthcare from Every Angle

### Mastek (2009-2013): Insurance & Early Product Thinking
Aswar joined Mastek as a Trainee Software Engineer in Mumbai, trained in Oracle Database and .NET technologies. He was deployed to US-based insurance billing software, working with companies like Amerisure, FCCI, and Nationwide.

He was promoted within the first year to Software Engineer due to his commitment and outcome-first approach.

### Advisory Board Company / Optum (2013-2019): Falling in Love with Product Management

**Why the move?** When Mastek wanted to send Aswar back to Mumbai, his mother was diagnosed with a brain tumor and needed medical attention in Chennai. He had to stay. When interviewing for new roles, he was selected by every company he applied to—Capgemini, CTS, HCL, Wipro, Polaris, and Advisory Board Company.

He chose Advisory Board because it was a **product company**, not a service company. Even then, his inner compass pointed toward building products.

**The Transformation:**
As a Data Analyst working on Crimson Market Advantage (CMA), Aswar analyzed hospital network performance, identified revenue leaks, and found growth opportunities for practices. He gained deep US healthcare functional knowledge.

Then he was assigned to Crimson Medical Referrals (CMR) and formed a 6-member integration team focused on HL7, Mirth Connect, Oracle, and Mulesoft—building integrations between systems for exchanging clinical information.

**The Moment He Fell in Love with Product Management:**

The Product Manager was stuck on a problem: provider directory information was outdated, leading to failed patient referrals and revenue loss for practices. He was trying to figure out how to build provider data cleanup *inside* the CMR application.

Aswar asked him one question: "Why do you think provider clean activity should be within CMR application? Why can't it be outside as a separate operational task that feeds data to CMR?"

That question unlocked everything. Aswar helped design a separate backend application for provider directory cleanup, operationalized it with an external BPO vendor (MiraMed Ajuba), and kept the customer-facing CMR application clean.

Customers saw clean data, less administrative burden, and could take action to reduce out-of-network referrals. Aswar won the **Delivery Excellence Award of the Year** at Advisory Board.

That was the moment he realized: *I can shape products. I can solve enterprise-scale problems. I can build things that create real impact.*

He was promoted to Senior Business Analyst, formed the first-ever Business Analyst team in Chennai (eventually growing to 10 members), and started playing a crucial role in product development alongside the Product Manager.

**Why he left:** When Optum acquired Advisory Board, the culture shifted. Advisory was employee-first, innovation-friendly, people-focused. Optum shut down Advisory products to promote their own, gave preference to Optum employees, and Aswar's learning stalled.

The moment he realized he wasn't learning anything new, he knew it was time to move.

### athenahealth (2019-Present): Proving He Could Rescue Products

Aswar joined athenahealth in 2019 as a Product Manager for the Interface product. This was a game-changer—he finally got to see the *other side* of healthcare. His previous experience was ancillary to EHR (CMA, CMR). Now he was inside the EHR itself, where doctors spend their time, where all healthcare data gets created.

**The Crucible Moment:**

Within 3 months, Aswar traveled to athena's US headquarters to transition ownership of the DIRECT Messaging product to Chennai. But when he arrived, the current Product Manager had been laid off. There was no knowledge transfer. No team. No roadmap.

Aswar walked into rooms filled with people from IIMs and IITs with far more experience. He felt inferior. A senior manager told him to "be assertive." That night, he couldn't sleep.

He remembered 9th grade. He remembered the Head Boy election. He told himself:

*"What has brought me here will not take me forward. There is something else I need to start focusing on."*

He spent 2 months observing, learning, adjusting. Then came his first leadership meeting—presenting product updates to athena's leadership team across all zones.

He delivered flawlessly. Answered every tactical question. His manager, the one who told him to be assertive, came up afterward and said: "I never expected you to present this well. Job well done."

That night, Aswar felt the same pride he felt when his 9th-grade teacher made the class congratulate him. Hard work delivers results.

**The Rescue:**

DIRECT Messaging was essentially abandoned when Aswar took over:
- Built in 2015 purely for compliance (MU2/MU3 requirements)
- 5% send success rate
- No funding since 2017
- No active development or team

Aswar formed a new scrum team in Chennai, talked directly to customers, connected with support and operations teams, and prepared a **3-year product roadmap** that was widely accepted by athena leadership.

**Results:**
- Year 1: Brought send success rate from 5% to 30%
- Eventually: 80%+ send success rate, 90%+ receive success rate
- Promoted to Senior Product Manager in 2021
- Replaced every fax touchpoint in athenaOne with DIRECT, saving massive fax costs
- Represented athena in DirectTrust forums, voiced concerns about directory data quality, participated in working groups (Directory, TIM+, Referral 360x standards)

**Beyond DIRECT:**

Aswar built **Electronic Case Reporting (eCR)** from scratch post-COVID—the largest eCR implementation in US healthcare due to athena's customer base connecting to almost all state HIEs. Completed development and production rollout in under a year.

He then tackled athena's biggest referral problems: fax-based referrals, split experiences, no tracking. After deep research with UX, he pitched leadership, got approval, recruited 12 contractors to scale a 15-person team, and delivered the **Referral 360x** product in 6 months (June-December 2025).

He presented mockups to 200+ participants at athena's Thrive conference—a standing-room-only session, one of the top presentations of the event. His first time presenting on stage to that scale.

## The Mindset Shift: Why Not India? Why Not the World?

During his tenure at athena, a question started forming:

*"If we can help fix healthcare problems for the US, why not India? Why not other countries? The problem exists globally."*

Aswar had:
- Tech background (engineering, databases, integrations, APIs)
- Product thinking (learned from world-class product companies)
- Strong healthcare domain expertise (15 years across insurance, data analytics, EHR, interoperability)
- Passion to solve problems
- Passion to become an entrepreneur

Since graduation, he'd wanted to build an enterprise-level software company—but it had to have **purpose**. His purpose: *Make healthcare delivery affordable, high-quality, and accessible for all.*

## The Validation: Tamil Nadu Government Award

Before fully launching TrueSpur, Aswar entered a competition from the Government of Tamil Nadu to design a mobile outreach vehicle for differently-abled persons in remote locations.

54 participants submitted designs, including well-recognized big MNCs in the design field who had already established their businesses. Aswar was just starting.

**His design won first place.**

**Why?** The big MNCs submitted designs without ground work. Aswar:
- Visited differently-abled hospitals to understand ground-level challenges
- Interviewed several physiotherapists
- Visited automobile coach factories to understand existing limitations

He designed based on real user needs, not assumptions. The mobile outreach vehicle he designed is now built and running in remote locations of Tamil Nadu, funded by the World Bank through the Tamil Nadu government.

This award boosted his confidence as an entrepreneur and instilled a powerful question:

*"Why can't I change and bring value to Indian healthcare? And if my idea fits the global market, why can't I build for the world?"*

## TrueSpur: Building World-Class Products from India

Aswar founded TrueSpur with a clear mission:

**Primary Mission:** Prove that world-class products can be built from India—not outsourced, not service-based, but true product companies that compete globally.

**The Model:** Hybrid product studio
- **Internal products:** Build our own healthcare products (Clinax, TrueBill, Halo, TafsirAI)
- **Client services:** Help non-tech founders turn their ideas into reality ("We craft, you lead")

**Flagship Product: Clinax (Telemedicine Platform)**

Aswar's vision: Hybrid healthcare delivery where patients in Tier 2/3 cities can visit a nearby PCP or small practice and do virtual specialist telemedicine alongside a skilled nurse—getting human touch from in-person care plus expert opinion from specialists on call.

Clinax is currently in MVP development:
- **Patient-facing:** Mobile app for health data management, ABDM integration (India's health interoperability layer), connection point to specialists
- **Provider-facing:** Virtual clinic + clinic management system for running practices and remote consultations

**Strategy:** Test and prove the model in India's booming healthcare market, then expand globally (including the US where Aswar has 15 years of domain expertise).

## The Vision: 2036

Aswar dreams of TrueSpur becoming a multi-billion dollar enterprise software company that creates 10,000+ jobs.

But success, for him, isn't just about the company metrics. It's about reaching a point where the business runs in autopilot mode so he can:
- Give back time to students and people in need
- Mentor founders and help them build their dreams
- Invest in startups and help them scale from idea to execution
- Serve as a board director for multiple companies, shaping communities and creating impact
- Spend time with his kids, helping them become self-dependent
- Create reliable, consistent sources of income for people in need to run their families

**The Covenant:**

"In order to help a lot, in order to give a lot, in order to upbring the society, I need to create wealth that I can share with people in need."

For the past 15+ years, Aswar has already been helping people with education fees, household needs, money, food, and shelter. TrueSpur is about scaling that impact—building an empire not to keep it, but to redistribute it.
