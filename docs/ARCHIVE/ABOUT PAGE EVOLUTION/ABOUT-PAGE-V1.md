## SECTION 3: THE FOUNDER'S JOURNEY

### Section Headline
**The Journey That Built TrueSpur**

### Subsection: The Failing Student Who Learned to Win

From 3rd grade through 9th grade, I failed subjects. Every exam. Every year.

But during those same years, I won the Moral Science Award—given to one student per grade for character, discipline, and how you treat people. Every single year.

In 9th grade, when I was made vice captain, I had a choice: keep failing and let everyone down, or face the reality that I had to change.

I faced it. I put in the work. For the first time in my life, I passed all subjects.

My class teacher made me stand in front of everyone and asked the class to congratulate me. That night, I made a promise to myself: *"I cannot afford to fail anymore. I have to be genuine to my efforts."*

I never failed another subject through graduation.

Then something remarkable happened: my fellow students elected me **Head Boy**—beating all the top academic rankers. In a school where only toppers typically won that position, they chose character over credentials.

**I learned:** Where you start doesn't determine where you finish. Capability is earned, not given.

---

### Subsection: Building Products, Building Teams, Building Systems

After graduating with a Computer Science Engineering degree, I spent 15 years building world-class healthcare products across insurance, data analytics, EHR systems, and interoperability.

**At Advisory Board Company**, I didn't just analyze data—I solved revenue leaks for medical practices. When the Product Manager was stuck trying to build provider directory cleanup inside the application, I asked one question: *"Why does this have to be inside the app?"*

That question unlocked everything. We built a separate backend application, operationalized it with an external vendor, and delivered clean data to customers without cluttering their workflow.

I won the **Delivery Excellence Award of the Year**. More importantly, I fell in love with product management.

But I wasn't just building products. I was building teams.

I formed the first-ever Business Analyst team in Chennai, growing it to 10 members. I created integration capabilities where none existed before. I built operational systems that scaled beyond me.

**I learned:** Building products is important. Building people and teams is transformational.

---

### Subsection: The Rescue Mission

When I joined **athenahealth** in 2019 as a Product Manager, I traveled to the US headquarters for knowledge transfer on the DIRECT Messaging product.

But when I arrived, the Product Manager had been laid off. No knowledge transfer. No team. No roadmap.

I walked into rooms filled with people from elite institutions with far more experience than me. I felt inferior. A senior manager told me to "be assertive." That night, I couldn't sleep.

I remembered 9th grade. I remembered the Head Boy election. I told myself: *"What has brought me here will not take me forward. There is something else I need to start focusing on."*

I spent 2 months observing, learning, adjusting. Then came my first leadership meeting—presenting product updates to athena's leadership team across all zones.

I delivered flawlessly. My manager came up afterward and said: *"I never expected you to present this well. Job well done."*

**The product I inherited was essentially abandoned:**
- Built in 2015 purely for compliance
- 5% send success rate
- No funding since 2017
- No active development

I formed a new team in Chennai. Talked directly to customers. Prepared a 3-year product roadmap that leadership accepted.

**The results:**
- Year 1: Send success rate from 5% to 30%
- Eventually: 80%+ send success rate, 90%+ receive success rate
- Replaced every fax touchpoint in athenaOne with DIRECT
- Saved massive fax costs for athena and customers
- Represented athena in industry forums, shaping interoperability standards

I also built **Electronic Case Reporting (eCR)** from scratch post-COVID—the largest eCR implementation in US healthcare. Completed development and production rollout in under a year.

Then I tackled athena's biggest referral problems. After deep research, I pitched leadership, scaled a 15-person team, and delivered the **Referral 360x** product in 6 months. I presented it to 200+ participants at athena's Thrive conference—a standing-room-only session, one of the top presentations of the event.

**I learned:** I could rescue abandoned products. I could build from scratch. I could compete at the highest level.

---

### Subsection: Beating the Big MNCs

Before fully launching TrueSpur, I entered a competition from the Government of Tamil Nadu to design a mobile outreach vehicle for differently-abled persons in remote locations.

54 participants submitted designs, including well-recognized big MNCs in the design field who had already established their businesses. I was just starting.

**My design won first place.**

Why? The big MNCs submitted designs without ground work. I visited differently-abled hospitals to understand ground-level challenges. I interviewed physiotherapists. I visited automobile coach factories to understand existing limitations.

I designed based on real user needs, not assumptions.

The mobile outreach vehicle I designed is now built and running in remote locations of Tamil Nadu, funded by the World Bank through the Tamil Nadu government.

**I learned:** Ground work beats assumptions every time. First-generation graduates can compete with and beat big MNCs. Outcomes matter more than pedigree.

## SECTION 4: WHAT WE BELIEVE

**What We Believe**

Your credentials don't define your capability. Your pedigree doesn't predict your performance. What matters is whether you're willing to learn, willing to work, willing to face the challenge instead of running from it.

This belief drives everything at TrueSpur. It's why we hire for learning capacity over degrees. Why we measure success by outcomes, not output. Why we do the ground work others skip.

**The pattern has proven itself true across 15 years:**

Failed student → Head Boy.  
First-generation graduate → Product leader.  
Unknown participant → Award winner.  
New Product Manager → Product rescuer.  
Employee → Founder.

Every transformation started from a position where I wasn't the obvious choice. And every time, the same truth emerged: **where you start doesn't determine where you finish.**

### Belief 2: Ground Work Beats Assumptions Every Time

The big MNCs submitted their designs without ever visiting a hospital. Without interviewing a single physiotherapist. Without understanding the real constraints.

I visited hospitals. I interviewed therapists. I studied automobile coach factories to understand what was actually possible.

I won because I did the work they didn't.

**The belief:** Assumptions are expensive. Ground work is cheap. Talk to users. Visit the site. Understand the constraints. Build based on evidence, not ego.

This is how we beat competitors who have more money, more people, and better brands. We do the work they skip.

### Belief 3: Character Compounds Over Time

For seven years, I won the Moral Science Award—given to one student per grade for character, discipline, and how you treat people.

For seven years, I also failed subjects.

When it came time to elect Head Boy, my classmates didn't choose the top ranker. They chose me. Because they'd watched how I treated people for a decade.

**The belief:** Character isn't a shortcut. It's a long game. How you treat people when no one is watching. Whether you do the right thing when it's hard. Whether you deliver what you promise.

Credentials open doors. Character keeps them open.

We hire for character at TrueSpur. Because character compounds. And over time, it becomes your competitive advantage.

---

### Belief 4: Outcomes Over Everything

At Advisory Board, I didn't just build a provider directory cleanup feature. I solved revenue leaks for medical practices. I reduced failed referrals. I created measurable value.

That's why I won the award. Not because I shipped code. Because I delivered outcomes.

At athenahealth, I didn't just maintain DIRECT Messaging. I transformed it from a compliance checkbox into a product that saved massive fax costs and improved patient care.

I didn't just design a mobile outreach vehicle. I built something that's now running in remote locations of Tamil Nadu, helping differently-abled people who couldn't access care before.

**The belief:** Output doesn't count. Features shipped don't matter. Hours worked are irrelevant. What matters is impact. What problem did you solve? What outcome did you create? What changed because you built this?

This is the standard at TrueSpur. We measure success by customer impact. By problems solved. By lives changed.

If it doesn't create real value, we don't build it.

---

### Belief 5: Face The Situation, Don't Run From It

In 9th grade, when I was made vice captain, I had a choice: keep failing and let everyone down, or face the reality that I had to change. I faced it.

At athenahealth, when I arrived in the US for knowledge transfer and discovered the Product Manager had been laid off—no team, no roadmap, no guidance—I could have panicked. Instead, I faced it.

When DIRECT Messaging had a 5% success rate and no funding, I could have maintained it and moved on to something more prestigious. Instead, I faced it.

**The belief:** The moment you run from a hard situation is the moment you stop growing. Every transformation in my life started by facing something difficult and refusing to quit.

This is what we do at TrueSpur. We don't avoid hard problems. We face them. Because that's where the real work is.
